## Grammar

### Simple Introduction

Use `Ik ben ...` to say your name.

Examples:

1. Ik ben William.
2. Ik ben Alexander.
3. Hallo! Ik ben Maxim.

Use `En jij?` to ask the same question back informally.

Example:

1. Ik ben Alexander. En jij?

### Basic Question: How Are You?

The board practices:

- `Hoe gaat het?`
- `Hoe is het met jou?`
- `Hoe is het met u?`

Useful answers:

- `Heel goed!`
- `Goed.`
- `Prima!`
- `Uitstekend!`
- `Het gaat wel.`
- `Niet zo goed.`
- `Niet zo best.`
- `Slecht.`

### Informal And Formal Address

The lesson introduces the difference between informal and formal forms:

- `jou` = informal "you"
- `u` = formal/polite "you"

Examples:

1. Hoe is het met jou?
2. Hoe is het met u?

## Pronunciation

### Consonants That Are Similar To Russian

The board notes that many consonants are close to Russian sounds, for example:

- `b`, `d`, `f`, `k`, `m`, `n`, `p`, `t`

Important beginner notes:

- Final `b` and `d` can sound less voiced, similar to Russian final devoicing.
- Dutch `r` can be pronounced in different ways depending on speaker and region.
- Dutch `l` is softer than hard Russian `л`, but firmer than very soft Russian `ль`.

### Short And Long Vowels

Dutch contrasts short and long vowels.

Short vowels often appear in closed syllables:

- `man`
- `kat`
- `kop`
- `put`

Long vowels can be written with double letters or appear in open syllables:

- `maan`
- `kaas`
- `been`
- `boon`

### Common Digraphs

The board introduces letter combinations where two letters make one sound.

Examples:

- `oe`: `koe`, `doet`, `boek`
- `ie`: `kiepen`, `dienen`
- `ei` / `ij`: `trein`, `kijken`

### Diphthongs And Letter Groups

Common combinations:

- `ui`: `huis`, `tuin`, `duin`
- `au` / `ou`: `blauw`, `bouw`
- `uw`, `ieuw`, `eeuw`: `duw`, `nieuw`, `leeuw`
- `aai`, `ooi`, `oei`: `taai`, `mooi`, `groei`

### Very Dutch Sounds

The board highlights sounds that may be difficult:

- `g` / `ch`: a guttural sound, for example in `graag`, `goed`, `school`, `schrijf`.
- `h`: lighter than Russian `х`.
- `ng`: nasal sound, as in `lang`.
- `sch`: common cluster, as in `school`, `schrijf`, `schip`.

### Reduction

The lesson mentions reduction of unstressed sounds, especially in endings.

Common examples:

- Final `-en` is often pronounced weakly or with the `n` dropped.
- Plural nouns and infinitive verb endings can sound reduced in normal speech.
